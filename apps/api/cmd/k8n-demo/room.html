Try k8n on a real Kubernetes cluster

One person at a time gets the whole cluster. Everyone else waits in a short queue. To use k8n with your own cluster, set it up on your machine. No queue, and nothing gets deleted.

Before you join

  • Everything is deleted when your session ends. That covers your apps, namespaces, Helm releases, saved workflows and history, and any AI key you enter. To keep your work, copy or download the YAML before the end.
  • A session lasts up to {{.Session}}. A bar at the bottom of the app counts down, and you can end it early.
  • Closing the tab ends your session after about {{.Idle}}, so the next person is not kept waiting.
  • Keep this page open while you wait. If you close it, you lose your place after a few minutes. When your turn comes you have {{.Claim}} to press Start; this page changes its title and, if you allow it, sends a notification.
  • Do not put anything private in it: no real passwords, keys or company data. The person who runs this server can see its logs.

What the server can handle

This runs for free on one small Oracle Cloud machine. Your session gets {{.Machine}}.

  • Fits well: a few Deployments and Services, ConfigMaps and Secrets, a database like postgres or redis, Jobs and CronJobs, autoscalers, the templates, and small Helm charts such as nginx or redis.
  • Will not fit: monitoring stacks like Prometheus and Grafana, Kafka, Elasticsearch, or anything asking for many gigabytes of memory. Those pods wait as Pending.
  • The first start of an image is slow, because the server downloads it. Images pulled before start in seconds.

What is switched off, and why

  • Apps cannot reach the internet. They can reach each other inside the cluster, and images still download.
  • "Open in browser" does not reach you here. Tunnels open on the server, not on your computer. Use logs, the terminal into a pod, and the live view instead.
  • No privileged pods, host paths or host ports, and the cluster's own namespaces are read-only. Charts that install cluster-wide pieces, such as CRDs or ClusterRoles, are refused.
  • Ingress objects are accepted but not served: there is no ingress controller on this cluster.
  • It is best effort: a free machine with no guarantees. If it is down, try later, or run k8n yourself.
